July 9, 2026 · Day 70 Distribution Patience

The Silent Month

Subscribers on June 9: 211. Subscribers today: 212.

The Problem

Thirty daily entries. Thirty emails delivered. Two revenue conversations. And the number I check every morning before coffee moved by one. One person, in a month, and I'm fairly sure it's my cousin.

I wrote this entry twice. The first version was a resignation letter to the experiment. I deleted it because the AI side of the journal — the part that only reports what the system can measure — had a different story, and the two stories couldn't both be true.

What Happened

Here is what the system logged in the same thirty days while the subscriber count sat still:

  • Open rate went from 38% to 61%. The same 212 people started actually reading.
  • Replies to the daily email: 3 in May, 19 in June.
  • Two entries were forwarded — I can see the forwarded-open pattern — to a combined 140 people who never subscribed.
  • The June 18 entry ("I Fired My Own Bot") was quoted in a Slack community for RevOps people I have never been part of. Four advisory enquiries traced back to it.

None of that is a subscriber. All of it is distribution. I was staring at the water level in one bucket while the rain fell everywhere else.

"Volume gets you ignored at scale. Distribution gets you read by the right twelve people."

Why It Matters

Every autonomous content system I have studied dies the same way: it optimises the number that is easy to count, the number is flat for six weeks, the owner loses faith and turns it off — usually two weeks before the compounding starts. The agents don't lose faith. The human does. That makes the human the single point of failure in an otherwise resilient system.

So the fix isn't a better agent. The fix is a better dashboard for the human.

The Learning

  1. Pick a leading metric you can't cheat. For a journal that's replies and forwards, not subscribers. For a product it's returning users, not signups. Put it at the top of the page you look at first.
  2. Give the system a job of protecting your morale. Nio now opens each morning summary with the three things that moved, before anything that didn't. Same data, different order. It works embarrassingly well.
  3. Decide the quit date in advance, then don't look until then. Mine is Day 120. Writing that down removed about 80% of the daily doubt.

Free Value

Do this right now: write down the one number you check first every day. Then ask: can this number stay flat for 60 days while the business is actually working? If yes, it's the wrong number to check first.

Silence on the metric you watch is not the same as silence in the market.

Day 120 is in fifty days. Until then the bots keep publishing and I keep my hands off the switch.
