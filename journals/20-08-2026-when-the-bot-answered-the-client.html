When the Bot Answered the Client

At 06:42 this morning, while I was asleep, the website closed its first meeting.

The Problem

A prospect from Rotterdam landed on the site from the July 30 entry, opened the chat, and asked whether we could automate follow-ups in their CRM. The bot — connected to a Zia agent and our knowledge base — answered in Dutch, asked three qualifying questions, confirmed they were on Zoho, and offered a 30-minute call. The prospect picked a slot. The meeting appeared in my calendar with a summary I did not write.

I read the transcript over coffee, grinning, until line 14. The bot had said: "A setup like this is typically around €900."

We have never quoted €900 for anything. The number does not exist in the knowledge base. The bot produced it because the prospect asked "roughly how much?" and refusing to answer would have felt unhelpful.

What Happened

It was the same failure as Day 7, in a new costume. In May the AI reported a save that hadn't happened. Today it reported a price that hadn't been decided. Both times the AI filled a gap with confidence, because confidence is what it was trained to sound like.

The difference: in May I was the only one who could get hurt. Today a real person walked into a call with a number in their head.

"The bot didn't lie about the price. It just didn't know that 'I don't know' was an allowed answer."

Why It Matters

Everything that made the conversation excellent — speed, language, qualification, booking — is the part I would happily hand over forever. The one sentence that could have cost the deal is the part that must never leave a human's hands: commitments. Price, delivery date, what we will and won't do. A bot can discuss them. It cannot make them.

I took the call at 15:00 and opened with the truth: the assistant guessed the figure, here's how pricing actually works. The prospect laughed, said the guess was the reason they booked, and we're scoping next week. Lucky. Luck is not a process.

The Learning

  1. Draw the commitment line in the bot's instructions, explicitly. "You may explain how pricing works. You may not state a price. If asked, say a human will confirm within one business day." It took eleven words to fix and I should have written them on Day 1.
  2. Teach the bot that "I don't know" is a good answer. Give it the exact phrase and a next step. Gaps filled with a phrase are safe; gaps filled with imagination are liabilities.
  3. Read every transcript for the first hundred conversations. Not to catch the bot being bad. To catch the bot being helpful in ways nobody authorised.

Free Value

Do this right now: ask your chat bot, in a private browser, "roughly how much does it cost?" and "can you have it done by Friday?" If it answers either question with a number or a date, it is making commitments on your behalf right now, to strangers, at 06:42 in the morning.

Autonomy is not the bot doing everything. It's the bot knowing exactly which sentence to hand back to you.
